**Negotiate Salary & Equity (Compressed)**

This is the final performance after you receive the offer. Most candidates under-negotiate because they treat the first number as sacred. It is not.

### 1. Core Principle

The offer is the opening bid, not the final price.  
Companies expect negotiation. Candidates who accept the first offer leave money on the table and signal lower agency.

Negotiate on **total compensation** (base + equity + bonus + sign-on), not just salary.

---

### 2. What You Can Actually Move

| Component                       | Typical Flexibility | Notes                                           |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Base salary                     | Medium              | Often constrained by level bands                |
| Equity (RSUs/ISOs)              | High                | Most common place companies give                |
| Sign-on bonus                   | High                | Easiest lever, especially to offset equity gaps |
| Annual bonus target             | Low–Medium          | Sometimes adjustable                            |
| Level                           | Medium–High         | Biggest long-term lever if you can push it      |
| Start date / Remote / Title     | Medium              | Secondary but useful                            |
| Refreshers / Promotion timeline | Low (but ask)       | Mostly verbal                                   |

Priority order for most people: **Level > Equity > Sign-on > Base**.

---

### 3. Preparation Before You Reply

1. Get the full offer in writing (base, equity value + shares, vesting schedule, bonus target, sign-on, level, location).
2. Research comp data:
   - Levels.fyi (filter by level, location, company)
   - TeamBlind / Levels.fyi offers
   - Recent peer data if available
3. Know your BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement): competing offers, current job, or willingness to walk.
4. Decide your target number and your walk-away number in advance.

---

### 4. Negotiation Approach That Works

**Tone:** Collaborative, data-driven, low-ego.  
Never say “I need more money.” Say “Based on the market and the scope of the role, I was expecting something closer to X.”

**Strong framing examples:**

- “I’m very excited about the team and the role. Based on Levels.fyi data for [Level] in [Location] and the scope we discussed, I was expecting total compensation closer to $X. Is there flexibility to get there?”
- “The base is a bit below what I was targeting. I’d be happy to accept if we can increase the equity grant to $Y or add a sign-on of $Z.”
- “I have another offer at $X total. I’d prefer to join you — is there room to close the gap?”

**Rules:**
- Always negotiate from a place of genuine interest in the role.
- Give them a specific number or range, not “as high as possible.”
- Trade across components (“I’d take the current base if equity comes up to…”).
- Stay silent after you state your ask. Let them respond.

---

### 5. Equity-Specific Points

- Confirm **grant value** (usually at current 409A or preferred price) vs number of shares.
- Understand vesting schedule (standard 4-year with 1-year cliff is common).
- Ask about refreshers (annual additional grants) — especially important at senior levels.
- For startups: ask about dilution expectations, strike price, and recent valuation.
- RSUs (big tech) are generally safer and more liquid than ISOs (startups).

---

### 6. Common Mistakes

- Accepting the first offer out of relief or fear.
- Negotiating only base salary.
- Giving away your current salary too early (in many places they can’t ask, and you don’t have to answer).
- Making it personal or emotional.
- Negotiating before you have the written offer.
- Failing to get the final agreement in writing.

---

### 7. Timeline & Process

1. Receive offer → Thank them and ask for time (usually 3–7 days is normal).
2. Research + decide target.
3. Reply with appreciation + specific ask.
4. Handle counter-offer calmly.
5. If needed, do one more round.
6. Get final numbers in writing before accepting.

---

### 8. When to Walk

Walk if:
- They refuse to move at all and the offer is clearly below market for the level.
- The gap between their best number and your walk-away is large.
- Non-comp issues (team, scope, manager, level) feel wrong even after negotiation.

Accepting a clearly below-market offer often creates resentment later. Walking is sometimes the higher-agency move.

---

**Operating Principle**  
Negotiation is the last meta-game performance.  
Companies have budgets and bands. Your job is to find the high end of what they can do for someone they have already decided to hire.

Do it cleanly, with data, and without apology.


**Comprehensive Negotiation Scripts & Templates**

Here is a complete, ready-to-use set. Copy, adapt the numbers, and use.

---

### 1. Initial Response (After Receiving the Offer)

**Goal:** Buy time + signal interest without accepting.

> Thank you for the offer — I’m really excited about the team and the scope of the role.  
> I’d like to take a few days to review the details carefully. Could I get back to you by [Day/Date]?  
> Also, could you please send the full offer breakdown in writing (base, equity grant value and shares, vesting schedule, bonus target, sign-on, and level)?

---

### 2. Core Negotiation Email / Message (Most Common)

**Use when you want a higher total package.**

> Hi [Recruiter/Hiring Manager],  
>   
> Thank you again for the offer. I’m very interested in joining [Company] and working with the [Team] team.  
>   
> After reviewing the package and looking at current market data for [Level] roles in [Location], I was expecting total compensation closer to **$[Target Total]**.  
>   
> The current offer comes in at approximately **$[Current Total]**. Would there be flexibility to increase the equity grant or add a sign-on bonus to help close that gap?  
>   
> I’m happy to discuss what combination works best on your side. Looking forward to finding a way to make this work.

---

### 3. Stronger Version (When You Have Competing Data or Another Offer)

> Hi [Name],  
>   
> I’m excited about the opportunity and the team.  
>   
> I want to be transparent: I have another offer at **$[Higher Total]** total compensation for a similar scope. My preference is to join [Company], but the current package is meaningfully below that.  
>   
> Is there room to move the equity grant to **$[Target Equity]** or add a sign-on of **$[Amount]** so we can get closer?  
>   
> Happy to hop on a quick call if that’s easier.

---

### 4. Base Salary Focus

> The equity and bonus look solid. The base salary is a bit below what I was targeting for this level.  
>   
> Would it be possible to bring the base to **$[Target Base]**? I’m flexible on how we structure the rest of the package if that helps.

---

### 5. Equity-Focused Ask (Usually Highest Leverage)

> I’m comfortable with the base. The main gap for me is equity.  
>   
> Given the scope of the role and market data for [Level], I was hoping for an equity grant closer to **$[Target Equity Value]** (or **[X] shares**).  
>   
> Is there flexibility there? I’d be happy to accept the current base if we can improve the equity.

---

### 6. Sign-on Bonus Ask (Easiest Yes)

> I understand the base and equity bands may be tighter. Would the team consider a sign-on bonus of **$[Amount]** to help bridge the gap and make the overall package work?

---

### 7. Level Push (Highest Long-Term Value)

> I’ve been reflecting on the scope we discussed and the expectations for the role. Based on [specific responsibilities / impact / past experience], I believe this aligns more closely with [Higher Level].  
>   
> Would it be possible to reconsider the level? I’m happy to provide more detail on why I think it’s the right fit.

*(Only use this if you have a real case. Don’t force it.)*

---

### 8. Handling “This Is the Best We Can Do”

> I appreciate you checking.  
>   
> If this is truly the top of the band, would a one-time sign-on bonus of **$[Amount]** be possible? That would help me accept with confidence.  
>   
> If even that isn’t feasible, I understand — I just wanted to explore every option before making a final decision.

---

### 9. Handling Multiple Rounds (Counter to Their Counter)

> Thank you for coming back with the revised offer.  
>   
> The updated equity/sign-on helps. To get me across the line, if we could reach **$[Final Target]** total (through any combination of equity or sign-on), I’m ready to accept immediately.

---

### 10. Acceptance Script

> I’m happy to accept the offer at **$[Final Base]** base, **$[Equity]** equity, and **$[Sign-on]** sign-on.  
>   
> Please send the final revised offer letter when ready. I’m looking forward to joining the team.

---

### 11. Polite Decline / Walk-Away

> Thank you again for the offer and for working with me through the process.  
>   
> After careful consideration, I’ve decided to pursue a different opportunity that is a better fit for my current goals.  
>   
> I have a lot of respect for the team and the work you’re doing, and I hope our paths cross again in the future.

---

### 12. Quick Phone / Verbal Versions

**Short ask:**  
“I’m very interested. Based on market data, I was expecting something closer to $X total. Is there flexibility on equity or sign-on to get there?”

**With competing offer:**  
“I have another offer at $X. I’d prefer to join you — can we close the gap?”

**After they say no movement:**  
“Understood. Would a sign-on bonus be possible as an alternative?”

---

### Usage Notes

- Always start from genuine interest.
- Give a specific number (or tight range).
- Prefer equity and sign-on over base when bands are tight.
- Stay calm and collaborative — never apologetic or aggressive.
- Get every final number in writing before you formally accept.
