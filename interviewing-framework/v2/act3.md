**Act 3 – Behavioral (Compressed)**

### 4 Master Story Types

These four story archetypes cover ~90% of behavioral questions across Amazon LPs, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and most other companies. Prepare 1–2 strong versions of each.

| #   | Master Story Type                            | Core Competencies It Hits                                               | Common Question Triggers                                                                  |
| --- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Ownership & High-Impact Delivery**         | Ownership, Deliver Results, Bias for Action, Impact, Customer Obsession | “Tell me about a time you owned something end-to-end”, “biggest impact”, “tight deadline” |
| 2   | **Leadership / Influence Without Authority** | Leadership, Mentorship, Earn Trust, Hire & Develop, Collaboration       | “Led a team”, “influenced without power”, “mentored someone”, “got buy-in”                |
| 3   | **Conflict / Disagreement**                  | Conflict Resolution, Have Backbone, Disagree & Commit, Earn Trust       | “Disagreed with manager/peer”, “handled conflict”, “pushed back on a decision”            |
| 4   | **Failure / Ambiguity / Learning**           | Learn & Be Curious, Humility, Dealing with Ambiguity, Resilience        | “Failed at something”, “made a mistake”, “unclear requirements”, “changed direction”      |

Prepare **one primary + one backup** for each type. That gives you 8 stories total — enough for most loops.

### STAR + Quantified Results + “I” Language That Lands

**Strict structure (stick to it):**

- **S**ituation (15–20 sec) → Brief context only. No long backstory.
- **T**ask (10 sec) → What *you* were responsible for.
- **A**ction (60–70% of the story) → What **I** did, step by step. This is the scored part.
- **R**esult (15–20 sec) → Quantified outcome + what you learned (if relevant).

**Language rules that actually score:**

- Heavy use of **“I”** (not “we”). Even in team projects, frame your specific contribution.
- Quantify everything possible: %, time saved, $ impact, latency reduction, headcount, conversion lift, incidents reduced, etc.
- End with a clear result, not a vague “it went well.”
- Keep total story length 1.5–2.5 minutes. Longer = lower score.

**Example skeleton (Ownership story):**

> “In Q3 last year our payment service had a 12% error rate during peak traffic (S). I owned reducing that error rate before Black Friday (T). I first… then I… I also… (A – 4–6 concrete actions). As a result we dropped the error rate to 1.8%, which prevented an estimated $2.1M in lost transactions over the holiday period (R).”

### How to Compress Messy Real Experience into Clean Narratives (Without Lying)

Real work is messy, political, and full of “we.” Interviewers want clean, linear, individual-contribution stories. Here’s the practical method:

1. **Pick the peak moment**, not the whole project  
   Choose the 2–4 week window where *your* actions made the biggest difference. Ignore the long messy middle.

2. **Assign clear ownership retrospectively**  
   If you drove the technical decision, the investigation, or the final push, say “I decided… I implemented… I convinced…”. You are not inventing credit; you are isolating your actual contribution.

3. **Cut supporting characters and politics**  
   Mentions of “my manager was unavailable” or “the other team was slow” usually hurt you. Collapse them into “I escalated” or “I unblocked by…”.

4. **Force a clean arc**  
   Real projects are: chaos → partial progress → more chaos → eventual success.  
   Rubric version is: clear problem → I took ownership → series of deliberate actions → measurable result.

5. **Quantify even when the real numbers were fuzzy**  
   Use ranges or conservative estimates if needed (“roughly 30–40% reduction”, “saved the team about two weeks”). Never invent precise fake metrics, but never leave results qualitative either.

6. **Pre-map every story to 2–3 competencies**  
   Before the interview, write the exact Leadership Principle or competency each story demonstrates. When the question comes, you already know which story to pull.

**Quick test before you use a story:**  
Can you tell it in under 2.5 minutes using mostly “I”, with at least one hard number, and a clear beginning-middle-end? If not, keep compressing.
