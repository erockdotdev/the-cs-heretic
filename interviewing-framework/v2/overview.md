### The Actual Structure of the Tech Interview Process

The process is not a truth-seeking evaluation of engineering ability.  
It is a **high-volume, low-resolution filtering system** optimized for three things:

1. Scalability (can be run by average interviewers on thousands of candidates)
2. Legal defensibility (standardized, documented, consistent)
3. Legible signal (easy to score, easy to compare, easy to justify)

Everything else is facade.

---

### Act 1 — Coding Round  
**Facade:** “We test problem-solving and computer science fundamentals.”  
**Reality:** A closed-book performance of ~18 pre-approved algorithmic patterns.  

Success condition: Recognize the pattern in <90 seconds, name it, execute the standard template, and narrate the expected script (brute force → optimize → complexity → edge cases).  

What is actually being measured: Pattern recognition speed + script fluency + composure under light pressure.  
What is largely ignored: Ability to solve novel problems, production debugging skill, code quality over time, or real architectural judgment.

---

### Act 2 — System Design Round  
**Facade:** “We test your ability to design scalable systems.”  
**Reality:** A box-drawing exercise against 7 canonical archetypes.  

Success condition: Map the prompt to the correct archetype early, hit the mandatory components (the “must-draw” boxes), perform the timed script (requirements → numbers → API → boxes → deep dive), and use the current approved vocabulary.  

What is actually being measured: Familiarity with the current fashion in distributed systems diagrams + ability to perform the 45-minute ritual.  
What is largely ignored: Whether the design would actually work in production, cost awareness, operational reality, or originality.

---

### Act 3 — Behavioral Round  
**Facade:** “We assess leadership, ownership, collaboration, and culture fit.”  
**Reality:** A narrative compliance test against a competency matrix.  

Success condition: Deliver 3–5 stories in strict STAR format that hit the company’s current keyword set, maximize use of “I”, end with numbers, and show the approved learning posture.  

What is actually being measured: Ability to select, frame, and perform personal history so it triggers the rubric.  
What is largely ignored: Actual past impact, honesty about trade-offs, or how the person behaves when there is no audience.

---

### The Meta-Game (What the System Actually Selects For)

Across all three acts, the process selects for people who are good at:

- Rapidly detecting which template is expected
- Performing that template with confidence and clean narration
- Suppressing complexity, ambiguity, and real-world messiness
- Speaking fluently in the current corporate dialect

It systematically filters *against* people who:

- Are stronger at real delivery than at performance
- Think in ways that don’t map cleanly onto the approved patterns
- Refuse (or are bad at) narrative self-compression
- Have deep but hard-to-package experience

---

From the front, it looks like rigorous evaluation of talent.  
From the side, it is a standardized theater production with a small number of approved scripts. The people who advance are those who have learned the scripts and can perform them on cue.

Here is the complete external view of the tech interview system as it actually operates in 2026, incorporating current patterns.

### The True Nature of the System

It is a **standardized performance filter**, not a truth-seeking evaluation of engineering ability.

It was designed for scale, consistency, and legal defensibility. It selects for people who can rapidly detect the expected template and perform it with clean narration and confidence. Real-world delivery ability is secondary and often poorly measured.

The correlation between strong interview performance and strong on-the-job performance remains weak to non-existent in the data. The process continues anyway because the alternative (deeper, slower, less standardized evaluation) is harder to run at volume.

---

### Current Structure of the Loop (2026)

Most mid-to-senior loops still follow some version of:

1. Recruiter / phone screen
2. One or more coding rounds (still heavily LeetCode-pattern based at most large companies)
3. System Design (weight increases sharply with seniority)
4. Behavioral / values / leadership (weight has grown significantly)
5. Hiring manager / bar-raiser / team match

**Key 2025–2026 shifts:**
- Pure algorithmic coding still dominates at mid-level and below at FAANG-scale companies.
- At senior and above, coding weight is declining relative to system design + behavioral depth.
- New formats are appearing: AI-assisted coding rounds (Meta leading), code comprehension, incident/log walkthroughs.
- Behavioral rounds now often take 30–40% of total loop time (up from ~10–15% a few years ago).
- Interviewers probe harder on behavioral stories because fabrication and over-polishing became obvious.
- Communication, structure, and process still outweigh raw technical correctness in scoring across all rounds.

### Act-by-Act Reality (Side View)

**Act 1 — Coding**  
Facade: “We test problem-solving and CS fundamentals.”  
Reality: Closed-set pattern matching + narration performance against ~15–20 high-frequency templates.  
What advances you: Speed of recognition, clean execution of the expected script, composure, and talking while coding.  
What is mostly ignored: Ability to solve novel problems, production debugging, long-term code quality.

**Act 2 — System Design**  
Facade: “We test your ability to design scalable systems.”  
Reality: Mapping the prompt onto one of a small number of approved archetypes and drawing the mandatory boxes while performing the timed ritual (requirements → numbers → API → boxes → deep dive on the expected bottleneck).  
What advances you: Correct archetype identification, hitting the expected components, fluent trade-off language, and driving the conversation.  
What is mostly ignored: Whether the design would actually work, cost reality, operational burden, or originality.

**Act 3 — Behavioral**  
Facade: “We assess ownership, leadership, collaboration, and culture fit.”  
Reality: Narrative compliance test against a competency matrix. Deliver polished STAR stories that hit the current keyword set, maximize “I”, end with numbers, and display the approved learning posture.  
What advances you: Story selection, framing, quantification, and delivery under probing.  
What is mostly ignored: Actual past impact, honesty about messiness, or real behavior when no one is scoring.

### What the System Optimizes For

Across the entire loop, the strongest predictors of advancing are:

- Fluency in the current approved templates and vocabulary
- Ability to perform under light-to-moderate pressure while narrating
- Confidence and clean structure
- Willingness and skill at compressing real (or semi-real) experience into the required format

It systematically disadvantages people who are stronger at real delivery than at performance, who think in ways that don’t map cleanly to the templates, or who resist narrative self-compression.
