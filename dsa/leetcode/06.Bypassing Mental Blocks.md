## Step 1: The Input Handshake (Topology & Constraints)

*Never look at the problem description text first. Look only at the data shape and $N$.*

### 1.1 The Topological Router

Ask: **What is the physical arrangement of the data in memory?**

* Contiguous row or sequence $\rightarrow$ **Family 1 (Linear)**
* Sorted values or overlapping intervals $\rightarrow$ **Family 2 (Sorted / Ranges)**
* Connected nodes, parent-child trees, or 2D coordinate grids $\rightarrow$ **Family 3 (Network)**
* Continuously updating or streaming elements $\rightarrow$ **Family 4 (Mutable / Priority)**
* Exponential combinations of choices $\rightarrow$ **Family 5 (State-Space)**

### 1.2 The Complexity Budget ($N$)

Check the constraint scale to lock your asymptotic target:

* $N \le 20 \rightarrow$ Budget: $O(2^n)$ $\rightarrow$ **Family 5 (Backtracking / Bitmask)**
* $N \le 10^3 \rightarrow$ Budget: $O(n^2)$ $\rightarrow$ **Family 5 (2D DP) or Family 3 (Dense Graph)**
* $N \le 10^5 \rightarrow$ Budget: $O(n \log n)$ $\rightarrow$ **Family 2 (Sorting / Binary Search) or Family 4 (Heaps)**
* $N \le 10^6 \rightarrow$ Budget: $O(n)$ $\rightarrow$ **Family 1 (Linear pass) or Family 3 (Standard BFS/DFS)**
* $N \ge 10^9 \rightarrow$ Budget: $O(\log n)$ or $O(1)$ $\rightarrow$ **Family 2 (Pure Math / Binary Search)**

## Step 2: The Algebraic Query (Structure & Monotonicity)

*Determine what structural properties you can exploit to avoid brute force.*

Ask three binary questions:

1. **Is there order or monotonicity?** (Can I establish a directional invariant where things only increase or decrease?)
* *Yes:* Enables Binary Search, Two Pointers, or Sliding Windows.
* *No:* Requires Hash Maps, Graphs, or Exhaustive Search.


2. **Are there overlapping subproblems?** (Does a future decision depend on a state already computed?)
* *Yes:* Enables Dynamic Programming (Memoization).
* *No:* Independent choices or raw traversal (DFS/BFS/Backtracking).


3. **Do elements arrive dynamically while queries occur?**
* *Yes:* Requires partial-order structures (Heaps, Tries).
* *No:* Static processing arrays.

## Step 3: State-Compression Mapping (Choosing the Mechanism)

*Map your algebraic answers to the precise micro-system tool.*

| Structural Reality                                        | The Chosen Mechanism                  | Why it works                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Contiguous window with monotonic constraints              | **Frame (Sliding Window)**            | Amortizes window expansion/contraction to $O(1)$ per step.          |
| Looking for historical sums/matches without nested loops  | **Ledger (Prefix Map)**               | Trades Space ($O(n)$ map) to eliminate Time ($O(n^2)$ loop).        |
| Searching sorted space or verifying a monotonic threshold | **Pivot / BS on Answer**              | Applies logarithmic pruning to discard $50\%$ of options per step.  |
| Deconstructing 2D ranges into timeline points             | **Sweep Line**                        | Collapses complex geometry into a 1D sorted event stream.           |
| Traversing node networks with cycles                      | **Graph DFS/BFS + Visited Set**       | Prevents infinite loops while tracking reachability.                |
| Dynamic maximum/minimum access under updates              | **Binary Heap / Monotonic Deque**     | Maintains partial order without full sorting overhead.              |
| Exponential future states with overlapping subproblems    | **Dynamic Programming (State Tuple)** | Compresses exponential history into polynomial equivalence classes. |

## Step 5: Runtime Safety & V8 Landmine Audit

*Before writing code, run this final syntax checklist to ensure zero V8 performance traps:*

1. **Memory Allocations:** Are there `.slice()`, `.splice()`, or `.filter().map()` chains inside loops? *(Refactor to pure index pointers to prevent hidden $O(n^2)$ scaling).*
2. **Map Safety:** Are we using `map.get(x) || 0`? *(Replace with `map.has(x) ? map.get(x) : 0` or `map.get(x) ?? 0` to prevent falsy zero bugs).*
3. **Sorting:** Did we write `arr.sort()` without a comparator? *(Always enforce numeric order: `(a, b) => a - b`).*
4. **Recursion Depth:** Is the recursion tree deeper than 10,000 frames? *(Convert DFS/Backtracking to iterative stacks if necessary).*
5. **Heap Integrity:** Do we need arbitrary deletion? *(Implement lazy deletion with a frequency map rather than trying to delete directly from the heap).*

### The Final Frontier: First-Principles Algorithm Design

When an interviewer invents a brand-new problem on the spot, they are testing whether you can *invent* an algorithm rather than just recall one. Elite engineers do this using three mental engines:

#### 1. The Induction Engine (Bottom-Up Construction)

Instead of trying to solve for $N$ all at once, assume you already have the optimal answer for everything up to $N-1$.

* *Ask:* If I am given the solution to the subproblem, what exact operation do I need to perform to extend it to $N$?
* This is how Dynamic Programming tables and greedy choices are mathematically derived from scratch.

#### 2. The Invariant Anchor (The Loop Contract)

When you are writing complex pointer, binary search, or backtracking code, don't write the code and hope it works. Write the **Invariant** first:

* *"At the start of every iteration, pointer $L$ is strictly less than all processed values, and pointer $R$ is strictly greater..."*
* If your code maintains that exact mathematical contract, the solution writes itself. Correctness is guaranteed by construction, not by testing.

#### 3. The Extremal Principle (The Extremum Argument)

If you are stuck on a proof or a greedy choice, look at the absolute worst or best possible case:

* *Ask:* What happens at the absolute boundary? If I take the maximum possible element right now, does it constrain or liberate my future choices? (This is how exchange-argument proofs for greedy algorithms are born).
